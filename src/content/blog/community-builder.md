I applied to the AWS Community Builder program mostly because I'd seen other people tweet about it and it seemed like a good way to get AWS credits. After a year in the program, the credits are the least interesting part.

## What the program actually is

Community Builders is AWS's way of recognizing and supporting people who create technical content — blog posts, videos, open source projects, talks, anything that helps other developers understand AWS services. It's not a certification or a formal title. You don't get a badge on your business card.

The categories roughly map to AWS service areas: Serverless, Data, DevTools, Security, Containers, etc. I'm in the Serverless category, which made sense given what I was already writing about.

## What you actually get

The tangible benefits are: AWS credits (enough to run meaningful personal projects), early access to some new services through private betas, and invitations to AWS re:Invent Community Builder events.

The less tangible benefit is the Slack community. There are a few thousand builders in there, and the signal-to-noise ratio is genuinely high. When I had a weird Fargate networking issue last November, I got three useful responses within an hour from people who'd hit the same thing.

## The application

The application asks you to link your content and explain why you should be in the program. I've talked to people who got rejected the first time and accepted the second — the difference was usually that their content catalog was thin on first application.

My honest advice: apply when you have at least 6-8 solid technical posts that are actually useful to other people. Not "here's how to create an S3 bucket" but "here's the thing I found after three hours of debugging."

## Is it worth it

Yes, if you're already creating content. No, if you're planning to create content just to get into the program. The credits are nice. The community is genuinely useful. But the best part has been that having the label makes me more accountable to actually publish — which is the hardest part of technical writing.
